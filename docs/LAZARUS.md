# LAZARUS: the origin bible (UPDATE 11, DECISIONS T-190)

This is the story truth for where the dead come from. `docs/CANON.md` §0 summarises it and wins on IDs and dates; this
file holds the full account, the people, the names, the sources and the line between what is real and what we made up.
Where an older document still says that Darkmatter *made* the dead, this file is right and that document is out of date
(`docs/STORY_CHANGES.md`, UPDATE 11, lists every passage that was changed).

**The one-paragraph version.** The dead come from a sickness that was kept sealed for a thousand years in a small
vessel that travelled with the relics of Lazarus of Bethany: what the monks who guarded it called *what death left
behind when it gave him back*. On Lazarus Saturday 1930 a district brigade closing a village church broke the seal.
The sickness keeps its own calendar: the dead rise on the fourth day. The Soviet state hid it (Laboratory No. 4,
"FOURTH DAY"), used it in East Prussia in October 1944, and lost its own dead to it. The Germans took the bodies to the one
place in the Reich that already understood what rises, Kaltenbrunn, where the Nachtglas crystal made the sickness fast
and the dust mills made it airborne (STAUB). Darkmatter Subscriptions LLC did not make any of this. It saw the awakening
from another galaxy, came to sell to it, packaged a culture of the sickness with its crystal, and explains only the
technology: the perk machines, the Crucible, the Ledger and Scrip, the Operator, the crate and Nachtglas itself.

**The rules that bind every writer (work order §1.1, §8):**
- **Faith is never the villain.** The miracle of John 11 is never mocked, faked, explained away or called a disease. The
  sickness is not the miracle. It is *the debt*: what death was owed and never collected. Keep that distinction explicit
  whenever the two are in the same paragraph.
- **The monks and Father Serafim are the most decent people in the saga.** The horror is entirely what men did when they
  broke the seal, and what men did with it afterwards.
- **Real history is the backdrop only**, accurate where it is used and labelled fiction where it is not (§12). No real
  person is a villain or a victim by name. No real atrocity site is a plot point. No real secret laboratory is ours.
- **Soviet and German soldiers are people.** The Returned were soldiers, and victims of their own command. Forced
  labourers and camp prisoners are **never** zombies and never spectacle: documents, empty bunks, a list of names.
- **Jakub is unchanged**: a person and a hero, never a joke, never a zombie.
- Nothing here changes a map, a system, a balance number, an Easter egg step or a Fernflug mechanic. It changes what they
  *mean*.

## 1. The relic and the vessel

### 1.1 What is real
Lazarus of Bethany, the brother of Martha and Mary, was four days dead when he was called out of the tomb (John 11:1–44;
"Lord, by this time he stinketh: for he hath been dead four days", John 11:39). The Orthodox Church keeps **Lazarus
Saturday**, the day before Palm Sunday, in memory of it, and gives him the epithet **Lazarus the Four-Days-Dead**
(Greek *ho Tetraēmeros*, Church Slavonic/Russian *Лазарь Четверодневный*). Tradition holds that he went to Cyprus, was
bishop of Kition (Larnaca), and died there a second time. In **890** a marble tomb was found at Kition inscribed as his.
The Emperor **Leo VI** had the relics brought to Constantinople; most accounts date the translation to **898** (some say
890), and the Church keeps the feast of the translation on 17 October. In return Leo paid for the church of St Lazarus
that still stands in Larnaca over the empty tomb. The Constantinople relics were lost after 1204.

### 1.2 What is ours (fiction, and never presented as history or as Church teaching)
With the relics, in 898, went a second thing, kept apart from them and never shown: a **small sealed vessel**, a
lead-sheathed glass phial no longer than a hand, closed with wax and a cord. The monks who guarded it did not call it a
relic and did not venerate it. Their book says what it was in one line:

> *what death left behind when it gave him back*
> (Church Slavonic, composed for the game: **є́же сме́рть оста́ви, єгда̀ возвратѝ є҆го̀**, *yezhe smert' ostavi, yegda
> vozvrati yego*)

Their understanding, which the game never contradicts and never confirms: when a man four days dead is given back, death
is owed something. The debt was not collected. It was *left*, in a cupful of what had been in the tomb with him, and it
has been waiting since. The miracle was the gift. The vessel is the debt. **They are not the same thing**, and nobody in
the game, not even the Operator, says they are.

### 1.3 The brotherhood of the lamp
A small brotherhood (the game calls them **the watchers**, Russian *Братство светильника*, "the brotherhood of the
lamp", fictional) kept the vessel in Constantinople for five and a half centuries, in a crypt, under a lamp that was
never allowed to go out. When the city fell on **29 May 1453** three of them carried it north: Athos, the Danube
principalities, Kiev, and at last the black-earth country of Muscovy, where by the end of the fifteenth century it came
to rest in the crypt of a wooden village church. The stone church that replaced it in the 1690s was built around the
crypt and dedicated to the saint the vessel had travelled with: **the Church of St Lazarus the Four-Days-Dead**
(*церковь святого Лазаря Четверодневного*), in the village the church named, **Chetverodnevo** (*Четвероднево*, from
*четверодневный*, "of four days"). The brotherhood thinned to a handful, then to the village priest and the men he
trusted, who took turns at the lamp. Their whole rule was one line, cut over the crypt door:

> **Є́же затвори́ся въ де́нь четве́ртый, никто́же да отве́рзетъ.**
> *Yezhe zatvorisya v den' chetvertyy, niktozhe da otverzet.*
> "What was closed on the fourth day, let no one open."
> (Church Slavonic, composed for the game; in modern Russian *Что закрыто на четвёртый день, никто не откроет*. The
> grammar is period-plausible (*еже* "that which", aorist *затворися* "was shut", jussive *да отверзет* "let him open",
> *никтоже* "no one") but the line is ours, not a quotation from any real inscription or service.)

What the watchers were: ordinary men, a priest, a reader, a bell-warden, a smith, who kept a lamp lit for five hundred
years over something they did not understand and did not want to, because somebody had asked them to. They are the
saga's definition of decency: **people who keep a promise they were not alive to make.**

### 1.4 Chetverodnevo
A village of some three hundred souls in the black-earth country, a long day's cart from its district town, which the
files call **Staryi Brod** (*Старый Брод*, "old ford"; fictional). One street, a pond, the stone church with its bell
tower and its lamp, the church-house where the priest lived, a cemetery on the rise. Which province is never said,
because the village is on no map now. Do not put it on one.

## 2. Lazarus Saturday, 1930

### 2.1 What is real
From 1929 the Soviet anti-religious campaign hardened. The League of the Godless added "Militant" to its name (*Союз
воинствующих безбожников*) and claimed millions of members. The law on religious associations of April 1929 put every
parish under registration and gave the authorities grounds to close churches. Bells were taken down and sent for melting
as scrap for industrialisation; the ringing of bells was banned in Moscow in 1930. Churches were closed by the thousand
and turned into storehouses, clubs and granaries, or dynamited; the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour in Moscow was
demolished on **5 December 1931**. Rural brigades, often local activists with a few police and a plenipotentiary of the
OGPU (the state security service until July 1934), arrived with a decree and a truck. A central decree in March 1930
reined in the worst local excesses, and did not end the campaign. All of that is history, told here without gloating:
the brigade are not monsters, and the believers are not saints. They are people in the same mud.

### 2.2 The calendar
In 1930 the Orthodox Pascha and the Western Easter fell on the same day, **Sunday 20 April** (7 April Old Style). So:
**Lazarus Saturday was Saturday 12 April 1930** by the state's Gregorian calendar, which is **30 March** by the Julian
calendar the Church kept and the village used. Palm Sunday was the 13th (31 March O.S.). The brigade's paperwork says
"12.IV.1930"; Father Serafim's diary says "the thirtieth of March, Lazarus Saturday"; both are the same day. Writers:
use the Old Style date in his mouth and the New Style date in every Soviet document.

### 2.3 The day, beat by beat (fiction)
1. **Morning.** The district brigade arrives in a truck: a chairman from the district soviet, a dozen activists of the
   League, two militiamen, and the OGPU plenipotentiary, **Arkady Volkov**, twenty-six, in a good coat. Red banners
   (plain cloth, no emblem on screen), a megaphone, a decree closing the church "for the needs of the grain campaign".
   The villagers kneel in the road. Nobody is struck. Nobody needs to be.
2. **The keys.** Father **Serafim Lebedev**, fifty-nine, at the church door, refuses to hand over the keys. He does not
   argue the decree. He says there is something below that must not be opened and that he will stand in front of it. They
   take the keys from his belt. He stands in front of the door anyway, until two of the men lift him aside, carefully,
   because he is old and because they are not, yet, the kind of men who do it otherwise. (Rule: no violence against clergy
   or villagers on screen. The scene cuts away; sound and aftermath carry it.)
3. **The bell.** The bell is roped down off the tower for the scrap quota. It falls the last four metres and cracks on
   the frozen ground. The sound it makes is the first sound of the horror. It is the only thing the village ever hears
   from it again, and it rings in the ambience of every map.
4. **The tower.** Dynamite. The bell tower comes down across the churchyard.
5. **The crypt.** Looking for church silver to requisition, the brigade breaks the crypt door. The lamp is still lit.
   The line is over the door; one of the activists reads it aloud and laughs, and does not know why it frightens him.
   There is no silver. There is a niche, and in it a small lead-and-glass vessel on a cloth.
6. **The altar step.** Volkov carries it up into the church, in front of everyone, to prove there is nothing in it. He
   breaks the wax with his knife on the altar step. **The blood inside is still wet.** It is a dark red, not black. A little
   runs over his glove and onto the stone. He wipes his glove on his coat. That is the moment the saga turns on.
7. **Night.** The brigade bivouacs in the church-house. Serafim sits in the ruined church with the vessel's cloth. He
   writes the first page.

### 2.4 The fourth day
The sickness keeps its own calendar, and it counts the way John 11 counts: a man dead on one day is raised **on the
fourth** (dead Friday, up Monday). Nothing hurries it and nothing stops it, until the Nachtglas (§4).
- **Sunday 13 April** (Palm Sunday): three of the brigade, the men who handled the vessel, are feverish. The brigade
  leaves for Staryi Brod with the vessel in Volkov's satchel; the three sick ride in the truck bed.
- **Tuesday 15 April**: the three die in the fever ward of the district hospital at Staryi Brod. Cause recorded: typhus.
- **Friday 18 April** (Great and Holy Friday): **on the fourth day they get up.** In the ward, at night. They bite. The
  ward's dead rise on *their* fourth day. The sickness is in the town.
- **In the village**, the brigade's first victims were not in the brigade: the feldsher **Matvei Grach** and the widow
  **Avdotya Pakhomova**, who had nursed the three sick men in the church-house on the Saturday night, took the fever from
  them and died on the 18th. The men of the village would not touch them. Father Serafim buried them himself, in the
  churchyard under the fallen tower, on Holy Saturday. He served Pascha in the roofless church on the 20th to whoever came.
- **Monday 21 April** (Bright Monday): **they come up out of the ground.** Everyone they reach follows on the fourth day
  after. By the end of the week the village is the sickness.
- Note the restraint: nothing rises *on* Pascha. The sickness keeps its own count; it does not keep the Church's.

### 2.5 The cover-up
- The OGPU seals the hospital on the 19th, the town on the 22nd and the whole district by the 25th, and records it as a
  **typhus quarantine**. Typhus was real and feared; nobody questioned a cordon.
- What happened inside the cordon is never written down. The one official line is in the district health report:
  *"Chetverodnevo: epidemic typhus, settlement liquidated by fire, 25.IV.1930."* The soldiers of the cordon were told
  not to let anyone out and not to go in. Some went in. (A single diary line, §2.6.)
- **Chetverodnevo is erased from the map.** The 1931 district map has a wood where it was. The 1939 census has no such
  place. Its people are a list of names in a file that is also a list of the dead.
- **Volkov survives**, because he was never bitten. He is the only man who saw the vessel opened and saw the fourth day,
  and he understands before anyone what the two have to do with each other. He wires Moscow in the cipher of his
  service. A **lead case** comes down with the cordon troops. He carries the vessel to Moscow in it on the train, on his
  knees, and does not sleep. He keeps the vessel for the rest of his life, because it keeps him alive: it is the one thing
  every man above him needs and none of them wants to touch.

### 2.6 Father Serafim's fate
One line, in his last diary page, and nothing else, ever. He is never a zombie on screen. He is never shown dying. The
line is written so that a reader can believe what they need to: §8 (the Archive) has it.

## 3. Laboratory No. 4 (the Soviets hide it)

### 3.1 What is real, and the one date that matters
The OGPU (*ОГПУ*) was the Soviet state security service from 1923. On **10 July 1934** it was dissolved into the new
all-union NKVD as its Main Directorate of State Security (GUGB). So **an NKVD laboratory can only exist from July 1934**,
and Volkov's career is written to fit: 1930–34 the vessel is an OGPU matter; from 1934 it is an NKVD one. The NKVD
really did run a secret toxicological laboratory in those years, known in the literature as "Laboratory No. 1" or
"the Kamera". **Ours is not it and is not modelled on it.** Laboratory No. 4 and its programme are fiction; the number
was chosen for the fourth day and for no other reason, and nothing in our files resembles the real laboratory's work,
staff or victims (§12).

### 3.2 The programme (fiction)
- **1930–34: "Object Lazar."** The vessel sits in a sealed store of the OGPU's operative-chemical section in Moscow
  under the file name **"Лазарь"** (*Lazar'*, Lazarus), with Volkov as its keeper. Cultures are drawn from it with a
  syringe through the wax; the vessel itself is never emptied and never seems to empty. Volkov's Report No. 1 (May 1930)
  is the founding document (Archive).
- **October 1934: Laboratory No. 4** (*Лаборатория № 4*), codename **ЧЕТВЁРТЫЙ ДЕНЬ, "FOURTH DAY"**, constituted under
  the GUGB with Volkov, now thirty, as its chief. A vault under a nondescript building in Moscow; a culture room; a
  "quiet ward"; a staff of eleven who never learned the whole of it.
- **Volkov's rise.** He rises through the purges of 1936–38 by being useful. Every chief above him is arrested in turn
  and he is not, because every new chief inherits the vault and finds he needs the one man who knows how to keep it
  shut. He is not a sadist and not a believer in anything. He is frightened, clever and ambitious, and the file is his
  life insurance. He is never a cartoon.

### 3.3 What they learned (the rules of the sickness, as the Soviet files state them)
1. **It lives in blood.** A culture taken from the vessel grows in blood and in nothing else. Dried, it dies; frozen, it
   waits. The vessel's own blood is still wet after a thousand years, which the files record and do not discuss.
2. **The dead rise on the fourth day.** A person who dies with the sickness in them gets up on the fourth day from the
   death, whatever killed them. Not the third, not the fifth. "The calendar," the staff called it.
3. **The risen spread it by bite.** The living who are bitten take the fever and die within days, and rise on their
   fourth. The living who are not bitten do not catch it from the air, in the Soviet experience: it is the Germans who
   change that (§5).
4. **The risen cannot be made to obey.** Not by voice, not by pain, not by the man they loved. They walk toward the
   living and toward noise. They remember one thing, and it is hunger. Every attempt at direction failed, and the
   failures are the worst documents in the file. The Germans change this too, with the Marke (§5.3).
5. **Fire ends it. Cold does not.** The cordon at Chetverodnevo had worked for one reason.
6. **It was always the same amount.** Nobody ever found the sickness anywhere except downstream of that vessel. "There
   is one source," Volkov writes in 1938, "and we have it, and I would give a great deal to be able to say that
   sentence and believe it."

### 3.4 Its victims
The test subjects from 1935 to 1939 were **prisoners from the camps**. The game never shows them as zombies and never
as spectacle. What it shows is paper: a transfer list with the camp's stamp, numbers and names, a line through each
name and a date four days after the one before it; a photograph of a hut with sixteen bunks, fourteen of them stripped;
a note in the culture log that reads *"Subject 31 asked for a priest. Refused. Noted."* The rule for every writer:
**the prisoners are a list of names, and the names are read with weight.** They had families. Some of the families
were told typhus.

### 3.5 The rule of last resort
By 1939 the laboratory's conclusion is written in one sentence on the cover of the file: **"It cannot be aimed. It can
only be released."** The programme is frozen. The vessel and the five sealed cultures that remain (the sixth, LZR-04, and
the priest's notebook went missing from a sealed box in 1938; §4) stay in the vault. A standing order,
signed by a man whose signature was later cut out of the page, makes FOURTH DAY **a weapon of last resort**, to be used
only on the direct order of the highest authority and only when the alternative is the loss of the state. Volkov keeps
the keys. He expects to grow old with them. He very nearly does.

## 4. Darkmatter's smaller job (the technology, not the dead)

Everything in `docs/CANON.md` §2–§5, `docs/FINEPRINT_LORE.md` and The Fine Print's chapters stays as built, with one
change of verb throughout: Darkmatter did not *make* the dead. It **found** them, **packaged** the sickness with its
crystal, and **sells** to them.

- **Why Darkmatter came to Earth at all.** The awakening of April 1930 was visible across the galaxy, in Darkmatter's
  instruments, as something that refuses to stay dead waking up on an unlisted world: a **"natural retention
  phenomenon"**, in Emerging Markets' phrase. For a company that sells retention, a species on which death is not a
  cancellation event is **the most valuable thing a market can have.** Sable Vantage's Market Opportunity Brief cites
  it as the first reason to open Sol-3. (The second and third, the local culture of ledgers and the substrate wake,
  stand as written.)
- **The 1938 probe and the crystal.** TM-SOL3-001 lands in the Kaltenbrunn salt on 9 November 1938, unchanged. Its
  crystal, **Nachtglas** (DARK-GLASS™), **does not make the dead rise.** What it does is *answer the sickness*: anywhere
  near it the sickness wakes faster and runs further, and a body with both in it can be reached, counted and, with a
  Marke, steered. The old product copy ("retention substrate; dead tissue near it keeps running its last impulse") is
  how Darkmatter describes that answer; Fasbender's *Nachhall-Hypothese* is how Kaltenbrunn describes it. Both are
  descriptions of the sickness in the presence of the crystal, neither of the sickness alone.
- **The culture in the probe.** Darkmatter shipped a **culture of the sickness along with the crystal**: on the
  waybill, *"returned inventory, lot LZR-04, origin Sol-3 / Laboratory No. 4 (Moscow), acquired by fold, backdated to
  arrival"* (Next-Era Delivery™ ignores chronology; CANON §2). Packaged so, the market arrives with its product already
  in it. The canisters ruptured into the glass room with the substrate.
- **Why Jan Pilch rose in 1938, on the fourth day.** Pilch and Bruno Hasse were in the glass room on the night of
  **9 November**, with the culture and the crystal. They were dead on the 11th, recorded as gas. They got up in the
  mortuary shed at **01:12 on 13 November**: the fourth day, if you count from the glass room, which is how Vendelin
  counted it (The Fine Print Ch. 2, kept as written); two days early, if you count from the death, which is how
  Laboratory No. 4 would have counted it and the first sign that **near the crystal the sickness does not wait its full
  four days.** Nobody on Earth knew both halves of that sentence until Reinholt did, in November 1944.
- **What Darkmatter knows.** Its telemetry counted the Erwachen as the best day in the market's history. It knows the
  sickness only as the "natural retention phenomenon" it came to sell to; its own documents never name Lazarus,
  Chetverodnevo or the vessel. The Fine Print's comedy is intact: a company that thinks it discovered a market when it
  walked into a graveyard.
- **The Loot Allocation Unit and the priest's notebook.** The fold that took lot LZR-04 from Laboratory No. 4's vault
  took the whole of what lay in the lot's box, Father Serafim's diary among it (Volkov had kept it as an exhibit;
  his 1938 vault inventory records it missing and cannot explain how). The Unit has been dispensing its pages ever since
  as "returned inventory", one at each site the market touched, with the last page on the shelf of the Moon relay's
  fulfillment store, where the field units racked one artifact from every market (§8).

## 5. Kaltenbrunn, Reinholt, STAUB and the Marke

### 5.1 Projekt Wiederkehr (1939 onward)
Unchanged: the Amt für Sonderforschung seizes the mine in March 1939; Dr. Augusta Reinholt founds Projekt Wiederkehr to
study the crystal and the miners it raised; she wants Liesl back; the perks, the Ledger and the Crucible come in through
Helvetia Automaten AG. What is new is the name her grief hangs on. **Lazarus** is a man four days dead who came home,
and she has read John 11 more often than she has read anything. Her programme's German name for what is in the raised
miners' blood, from the first sample she cultured in 1939, is **Lazarusblut** ("Lazarus-blood"). She does not know
where it came from. She knows it is *in* them, and that the crystal alone does not raise a body that has not got it.

### 5.2 What the Soviets did first: October 1944
**Real history.** The Red Army first crossed onto German soil in East Prussia in the **Gumbinnen–Goldap operation,
16–30 October 1944** (3rd Belorussian Front). Gumbinnen was taken on the 22nd and retaken by the Germans on the 24th;
the offensive stalled against heavy resistance and was broken off at the end of the month. Our fiction begins where the
real offensive stalls, and it names **no real place as a scene** of what follows: the sector in the files is "the line
between Gumbinnen and Goldap, Point 112", and the villages in the cutscene are unnamed.

**Our alternate history.**
- **22–24 October.** The attack stalls. Moscow sends Volkov, now forty, and the last-resort order, which has been waiting
  five years in a safe.
- **26 October, before dawn.** **FOURTH DAY is released** on the German line two ways: by **shell** (thin-walled
  chemical rounds with a wet charge, which hit and do not explode properly, and are mistaken for duds), and by **the
  blood of infected volunteers**, men of a penal company who were given the sickness and sent across the line as
  "infiltrators", knowing less than they were owed. The front report calls them volunteers. The file has their names.
- **It works.** The German line breaks on the 29th–30th, when its dead get up in their own trenches.
- **Four days later** the Red Army's own dead start getting up: from field graves, aid stations and the first wet snow,
  **thousands of them**, every man who had died of anything since the release with the sickness in him. They do not know
  whose side they were on. **They walk west**, toward the guns, toward the living. The Red Army is fighting the Germans
  and its own risen at once. The soldiers' word for them is **вернувшиеся**, *vernuvshiesya*, **"the Returned"**.
- **The cover-up again.** Moscow buries it. Front reports call it **"an epidemic among the enemy"**. The men who saw it
  write home in a different hand (Archive: the front report).
- **The war on Map 0.** In this history the line broke and kept breaking, so by December the front is far west of where
  it was in life. **The distant war seen from the Crossroads Inn**, the guns on the eastern horizon, the flares, the
  burning town, **is the front where the Soviet dead are walking.** The Red Army is holding it against both.

### 5.3 The Germans make it airborne
- **The trains.** The Germans are quick. From the first week of November 1944 infected Soviet dead, recovered from the
  broken line and put down with fire where they could be, are shipped in sealed goods wagons to Kaltenbrunn, the one
  place in the Reich that already understands what rises. **Fasbender saw them come in on the trains**, at the rail
  siding, at night, and it is the thing that finishes him (`docs/gravelight/CHARACTERS.md`).
- **What Reinholt's laboratory finds** (her STAUB memos, Archive):
  1. Lazarusblut and the Soviet "Lazar" are **the same sickness**: her 1939 culture from the raised miners and the
     culture from the Soviet dead are indistinguishable. She works out the probe-culture half of it from the German side
     and never learns the Moscow half.
  2. **Near Nachtglas the sickness no longer waits four days. It takes minutes.** The nearer the Heart, the fewer.
  3. **Ground fine with the crystal into a violet dust, it carries on the air.** A body that breathes the dust has
     the sickness and the crystal both, and rises in minutes when it dies, and can be *reached* (§5.4). Abteilung III's
     mills, built for the tonics, are retooled. The programme is **STAUB** ("dust"), and the plan is to send it back east.
- **The Marke gets its new reason** (kept from U09, `docs/CANON.md` §2.2; everything there stays). From November 1944
  Abteilung III sells the troops the chip under the skin of the right hand or the forehead, **"die Marke, die dich
  hält"**, with a second line on the poster: *Schutz gegen die Lazaruskrankheit*, **protection against the Lazarus
  sickness**. It is not protection. It is a Nachtglas tag. A marked body that dies rises **on command** and **walks where
  the relay points it**: the one thing Laboratory No. 4 said could not be done, done with a grain of crystal and a
  carrier wave. Deacon still knows it from Revelation 13, a line at a time. The labourers were never offered it. Jakub
  refused it in 1943. Nothing in §2.2 moves.

### 5.4 20 December 1944: the Erwachen
It was **the first STAUB release**, from the rocket pad above Kaltenbrunn, aimed at the Soviet front. The weather report
was wrong. The wind turned at 22:40 and blew the dust back over Kaltenbrunn, the villages and the road garrison. **That
same night Reinholt drove the Heart to full resonance to reach Liesl.** Both things are true at once, and they fit the
canon as built: every body within thirty kilometres rose, Soviet and German alike, the marked on the carrier and the
unmarked on the dust and the Heart's answer. The signals section at the Kreuzweg logged one carrier on every band at
23:02 (`INT_M0_10`). Three nights later the violet light from the same Heart brought down GRAVELIGHT ONE (unchanged).

  everyone.
- **Fernflug (May 1945).** The Kranich's bombs carry STAUB across the Atlantic. At Groom Lake the Allies have their
  own programme, built on **samples Peg's orders bought** (Res's 1943 vial, the Totenhafen crate, the Nevada stone). The

  1953 card does.
- **The Moscow 1953 epilogue card** (after the Fernflug canon ending; `docs/MAP4_FERNFLUG.md` §12.11): a vault under
  Laboratory No. 4. A lead case on a steel table. An old man's hand resting on it; the hand is Volkov's, forty-nine and
  older than that. The case is open. **The vessel is still there, and it is still wet.** Dread, not a sequel advert:
  no voice, no music, the lamp in the frame is electric and it flickers.

### 5.6 The fourth-day rule in play (T-192's Hard Truth)
A teammate who bleeds out rises after four seconds, a nod to the fourth day, and must be put down. The canon reading:
by 1944 everyone on these maps has breathed the dust, so the sickness is in them and the crystal is in them, and near
a Darkmatter site it takes seconds. The respawn rules do not change; Jakub never dies in canon and is never a zombie.

## 6. The people

### 6.1 Father Serafim Lebedev (отец Серафим Лебедев), b. 1871
Priest of Chetverodnevo since 1899, a widower, a reader of John and of the weather. The last keeper of the lamp. He
knows what the vessel is said to be and does not pretend to know more. He is not brave in the way soldiers are; he is
brave in the way of a man who stays where he was put. His **diary** is the thread through the whole game: one Archive
page per map, in order, written before and during the awakening, read aloud in Russian with English subtitles
(`SER_DIARY_<MAP>_01`). He is never a zombie on screen; his fate is one line (§8). His voice: unhurried, plain, a
village priest's Russian with no church-Slavonic ornament except when he quotes. He never preaches at the reader. He
notices things: the thaw, the cracked bell's note, the colour of the blood.

### 6.2 Arkady Volkov (Аркадий Волков), b. 1904
A Tula factory boy, a Komsomol activist, an OGPU plenipotentiary at twenty-six, chief of Laboratory No. 4 at thirty.
The man who opened the vessel and ran FOURTH DAY. **Frightened, clever, ambitious, and never a cartoon.** He keeps the
vessel because it keeps him alive. His reports are precise, dry and honest to a fault, because the file is the only
place he can be honest. He is present in documents on every map and in the October 1944 cutscene (T-191), where he
watches through binoculars and says very little. In 1953 his hand is on the case.

### 6.3 The squad (existing secrets and arcs kept; `docs/gravelight/CHARACTERS.md`)
- **Deacon** (a preacher's son): the saga's quiet theological voice. Lazarus, the Mark, "the last enemy that shall be
  destroyed is death" (1 Cor. 15:26). **No sermons.** A line at a time, usually to a machine, usually when nobody asked.
- **Wren** (Home Army): knows the NKVD, from the couriers who came west through the Soviet zone in 1940–41 with their
  stories, and from what her cell learned a Soviet "typhus cordon" could mean. She recognises the Soviet files' tone
  before she can read them. Her brother's story is unchanged.
- **Dutch:** his grandmother's letter is about the glass in the salt, as now. Nothing in it changes. What changes is what
  he hears in it: *he would not stay down.*
- **Peg:** Hale's orders now say **retrieve samples of the sickness and the crystal**, and the tokens. London learned of
  the sickness from the Soviet "epidemic" reports in November 1944 and from Res's 1943 note, and Directive LANTERN was
  amended the week before the drop.
- **Jakub:** unchanged.
- **Fasbender:** more haunted. He saw the Soviet dead come in on the trains, and he knows which of the sealed wagons at
  the siding are still closed.

## 10. Tone and craft rules for everyone writing in this layer
- **Period voice, restraint, dread over gore.** A sheet that moves. A list with lines through it. A bell that cracked.
- **Never explain the miracle.** Serafim does not theorise. Volkov does not theorise. Reinholt theorises about the
  crystal, never about Bethany. The Operator talks about "the fourth day" and "the returned" with a salesman's warmth and
  never says "Lazarus"; the word belongs to the people who mean it.
- **The sickness is the debt, not the gift.** If a line could be read as "the resurrection was a disease", rewrite it.
- **The brigade, the OGPU cordon, the Red Army, the German garrison are people.** Frightened, obedient, sometimes
  decent. The horror is what they were told to do and did.
- **Russian is spoken, English is read.** Serafim, Volkov, the Soviet radio and the Returned speak Russian (`speech`) with
  an English subtitle (`text`), contract C3.
- **No real Nazi symbols, no political imagery for shock.** Soviet uniforms period-correct and plain. Red banners are
  plain cloth. The Sunburst-and-Split-Bell stays Wiederkehr's emblem.

## 12. Sources (the real history leaned on, checked 2026-11-07)
- **John 11:1–44** (the raising of Lazarus; "four days", 11:17 and 11:39). Any standard text.
- **Lazarus of Bethany in Orthodox tradition; the Kition tomb and the translation:** OrthodoxWiki, "Lazarus"
  (https://orthodoxwiki.org/Lazarus); Wikipedia, "Church of Saint Lazarus, Larnaca"
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Lazarus,_Larnaca); John Sanidopoulos, "Synaxarion of the Translation
  and Placement of the Relics of St. Lazarus" (https://johnsanidopoulos.com/2017/10/synaxarion-of-translation-and-placement.html).
  The sources agree on 890 for the discovery and mostly on 898 for the translation under Leo VI; one gives 890 for the
  translation. We say "898 (some say 890)".
- **Lazarus Saturday and the 1930 date:** Orthodox Easter 1930 fell on 20 April (https://truecalendar.com/orthodox-easter-dates/1930),
  the same day as the Western Easter; the Julian computus for 1930 (golden number 12, Paschal full moon 4 April O.S.)
  gives the same. Palm Sunday 13 April, Lazarus Saturday 12 April, Gregorian; 31 and 30 March Julian.
- **The Soviet anti-religious campaign, bells and closures:** *Christian History* magazine, "Melting down the bells:
  Orthodox and atheists in Russia" (https://christianhistoryinstitute.org/magazine/article/melting-down-the-bells-orthodox-and-atheists-in-russia);
  Encyclopedia.com, "League of the Militant Godless" (https://encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/league-militant-godless);
  Wikipedia, "Persecution of Christians in the Eastern Bloc" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians_in_the_Eastern_Bloc);
  contemporary press (Dominion, 28 Dec 1929, "Propaganda against religion", on the bell-melting campaign:
  https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/DOM19291228.2.52). The standard scholarly account is Daniel Peris,
  *Storming the Heavens: The Soviet League of the Militant Godless* (1998). The Cathedral of Christ the Saviour was
  demolished on 5 December 1931.
- **The OGPU and the NKVD:** Wikipedia, "Joint State Political Directorate" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_State_Political_Directorate)
  and "Chronology of Soviet secret police agencies" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology_of_Soviet_secret_police_agencies):
  the OGPU was dissolved into the all-union NKVD on 10 July 1934 as the GUGB. The real toxicological laboratory
  ("Laboratory No. 1", "the Kamera") is documented in Wikipedia, "Poison laboratory of the Soviet secret services"; it
  is cited here only so that nobody mistakes ours for it.
- **East Prussia, October 1944:** Wikipedia, "Gumbinnen Operation" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gumbinnen_Operation),
  citing Frieser et al. (2007); G. Kretinin's paper on the operation (https://balticregion.kantiana.ru/upload/iblock/720/Kretinin%20G..pdf),
  which ends it on 27 October where Wikipedia says 30 October; Warfare History Network, "Goldap Operation: Soviets in the
  Prussian heartland" (https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/article/goldap-operation-soviets-in-the-prussian-heartland/).
  We use 16–30 October, Gumbinnen taken 22 Oct and retaken 24 Oct, and name no real locality as a scene.
- **Church Slavonic.** The two lines are composed for the game in a period-plausible register (§1.2, §1.3); they are not
  quotations. Anyone with better Slavonic is welcome to correct the diacritics; the words stand.
