# T-265: what the game's own scripts cost per frame, November 2026

Branch `claude/perf-tick`, cut from master `c5d5b5e0`. Not merged, not pushed.

## What was asked

The owner: "we need to optimize, we need to make this playable on macOS SteamOS. the frame rate still sucks at
times." This part (T-265) is the CPU cost of the game's own scripts per frame and the hitches, so the game holds
its frame rate on a much slower CPU than the owner's desktop. The ports and presets are T-264, the render load
T-266.

The aim set for it: game logic at round 15 on Map 0 at or below about 4 ms a frame on this desktop (headless),
no script-caused frame over 16.7 ms in a `--hitch` run, and no map or late-round situation much worse than Map 0
round 15.

## In short

- **The aim was not met.** Map 0 at round 15 cost 9.1 ms of scripts a frame (12.3 ms a frame with the engine's
  own work, nothing drawn). Standing still in the ring of 24 it now costs about 4 % less (four interleaved pairs on the loaded machine, each run's load taken out: -5 %, -7 %,
  -3 %, -3 %). A played round costs about 10 % less (pairs: -14 %, -22 %, -6 %, +5 %). The target was about 4 ms.
  What is left is the engine's work called from the scripts (the zombies' `move_and_slide` and animation trees,
  about 3 ms between them for 24 bodies) and the bots (0.55-0.65 ms a brain a tick): neither shrinks without a
  decision that is the owner's (the physics engine, the squad's size on a low preset, a 30 Hz tick). The list is
  under "What is still expensive".
- **What the frame gained** is mostly for a player who moves. In a played round (`m0play`: the agent plays the
  survivor and trains the horde, so half the zombies are behind the camera) the zombies' animation steps fell
  from 77 % to 39 % of the zombie-frames and their foot rays from 67 % to 24 % of the ticks; `zombie.gd` went
  from 4.6 to 2.8 ms a frame in that pair of runs (the frame's scripts 12.4 -> 10.7 ms). Standing still in a ring
  of 24, the old benchmark, nothing is out of view and the gain is the small scripts only: the bots' navmesh
  asks, the squad panel, `game.gd`, the day cycle, the horse.
- **Hitches: the first-use ones are gone, counted, on every map.** No sound is loaded in the frame that first
  plays it once the game has been up for 4 seconds (before: several a round, 16-30 ms each, the worst frame of
  four maps). A voice line heard for the first time waits for a loader thread instead of loading in its frame
  (before: 20-44 a round at 4-12 ms each; 2,681 lines). The match's first kill no longer draws a decal texture
  in script (26 ms) or compiles the dog tags' script (14 ms): the two largest parts of the "first shot" spike
  T-256 left open (22-44 ms at the same second of every run; the kill's other listeners were under 1.5 ms each
  afterwards but for `FirstHints` at 8 ms once, on the loaded machine, not followed up). No whole-map node search
  runs during play (3.7 ms each: one every 6 s for the bots' errands, one at every power-up). `perf_marks --diag` now lists every resource that
  comes into the engine's cache during play: on the seven maps and the live round 30 it lists voice files on
  loader threads and nothing else.
- **Hitches that are left** are computations, not loads: a single bot's think or errand pick (5-22 ms), a relief
  squad fielded, several deaths in one frame (a ragdoll is 23 nodes built on the spot). No script-caused frame
  over 16.7 ms is **not** achieved.
- **Round 30 and the tank, noon, the horse, the locker:** none is a problem of its own. The Sherman covering the
  yard is 0.15-0.25 ms a tick; round 30 is dear because 40 zombies are up at once. The day cycle wrote its eleven
  values to the renderer every frame and now writes none while the hour stands. The horse at its rail was
  0.19 ms; the locker shows in no list. **Not measured: a player riding the horse, a player driving the tank.**
- **On a Steam Deck** (an estimate by the 2.5-3 x rule, nothing run there) the game's own logic with a full squad
  at round 15 does not fit: the 60 Hz tick alone takes 16-19 ms of every 16.7 ms. See the estimate's table and
  what would change it.
- **All timings here were taken on a machine that two other workers kept busy.** The claims rest on counts
  wherever a count exists; the before / after times are interleaved pairs and say so where the pair was noisy.

## How it was measured

Everything is headless (no window may be opened on this PC): `perf_marks --hitch` at 60 fixed frames a second,
so one frame is one physics tick plus one process step and its wall time is the CPU work of a 60 FPS frame
without the renderer. `tools/tick_perf.sh <tag> <scenario> [runs]` runs one named scenario;
`tools/tick_perf_table.py <dir>` prints the medians.

| Scenario | What it is |
|---|---|
| `m0` `m1` `m2` `m4a` `m4b` `m4c` `m4d` | each map at round 15: 24 zombies kept alive round the standing player, 3 bots + Jakub + the map's companion, 30 s |
| `m0r30` | Map 0 round 30, the Sherman parked and covering the yard (machine gun and 75 mm), 24 zombies |
| `m0r35` | Map 0 round 35 |
| `m0noon` | Map 0 with the day held at noon |
| `m0corpses` | Map 0, four of the 24 killed and replaced every second (bodies, ragdolls, gore, the director's refill) |
| `m0live` | Map 0, a live round 15 (kills, spawns, the squad can go down) |
| `m0boss` | Map 0, a live Foreman round |
| `m0live30` | Map 0, a live round 30 for 60 s: the Sherman's arrival and the assault (40 of the dead, 10 burning) |
| `m0play` | Map 0, a live round 15 for 60 s with the player's own survivor played by the agent (`--botmode`): a moving camera with the horde behind it. The nearest thing to a person playing that a headless run has |

**The machine was quiet for the first hour only.** Other workers ran exports, smokes, test suites and soaks on
it all evening (and so did this work, at the end), and the same build's frame read 12 ms in the first hour and
25-43 ms later (the tool's own noise floor, a fixed 0.1 ms loop timed every tick, went from a worst of 0.12 ms
to 5-59 ms). So:

- the baseline survey is from the quiet hour, one run a scenario; the before / after at the end is interleaved
  pairs (A, B, A, B ...) in a second worktree, with each run's load taken out as described there; the numbers in
  between (in the commit messages) are single runs and say so;
- where a change can be **counted**, the count is given and is what the claim rests on: animation steps, foot
  rays, navmesh asks, files loaded in the frame that needed them, nodes built per frame, resources that came into
  the engine's cache during the window. `perf_marks` gained these counters; a count does not depend on how busy
  the machine is;
- a time difference under about 10 % with no count behind it is called noise.

New in `perf_marks` (its default run is unchanged): `--tank`, `--hour=H`, `--boss`, `--corpses`, `--probe-all`
(the probe walks the whole tree, autoloads included, and splits the frame's time outside the scripts into the
gap after the physics scripts and the gap after the process scripts), `--vo-async`, `--diag` (the cost of a
whole-map `find_children`, what is animating, every resource first loaded during the window), `--ab=no_view_lod`,
the work counters, the frames that built the most nodes.

## What changed

One commit each, in the order they were made; any one can be reverted by itself (8 with its fix, 10 with its
follow-up; 7 and 12 use the view helpers 1 added to `zombie.gd`, 13 the ones 12 added). "Counted" numbers do not
depend on the machine's load; times are single headless runs or interleaved medians as said.

### 1. Zombies out of the camera's view animate every 4th frame and cast no foot rays (`b2b9f3ab`)

- **Was:** the animation LOD went by the distance from a zombie to its own target. Every zombie behind the
  player's back, and a whole horde round a bot 40 m from the camera, was animated every frame: the tree's
  advance (0.05 ms), the pose code round it (0.02 ms), the skeleton's modifiers (0.012 ms) and four foot rays a
  tick (0.046 ms).
- **Now:** with a camera the distance is the camera's, and a body outside the view cone (the screen's corner
  angle plus 10 degrees, from the camera's own field of view and the picture's aspect) and more than 3 m away is
  stepped every 4th frame with the time it skipped, exactly as bodies beyond 35 m always were. Its foot rays are
  not cast; the IK weight eases back in a quarter of a second once it is seen. Without a camera nothing changes.
- **Safe because:** the body, its hitboxes' place, the attack and every gameplay timer are on the physics tick
  and are not touched. Footsteps and animation events still fire (up to 3 frames late, out of view).
  `Zombie.view_lod = false` switches it off (`perf_marks --ab=no_view_lod`).
- **Counted** (`m0play`, 60 s, same build, switch off -> on): animation steps 77 % -> 39 % of the zombie-frames
  (15.1 -> 7.6 a frame); ticks with foot rays 67 % -> 24 %; 49 % of zombie-frames were out of view.
  `zombie.gd` `_process` 1.46 -> 0.83 ms, `_physics_process` 3.11 -> 2.00 ms in that pair of runs.
- **Where it does nothing:** standing still in a ring of 24 (the old benchmark, `m0`), every zombie is within
  3 m or in front. The gain is a moving player's.

### 2. A walking bot asks the navmesh under it 5 times a second (`94b16fc9`)

- **Was:** `BotLocomotion._tick_stuck` called `NavigationServer3D.map_get_closest_point` every tick a bot wanted
  to move (the marooned-on-furniture and beside-the-navmesh checks): 0.07 ms a call, the same call T-256 took out
  of the zombies' chase.
- **Now:** every `NAV_LOOK_S` (0.2 s); each look counts the time since the last, so the drop still comes
  `drop_after_s` and the snap `off_mesh_snap_s` after the trouble began, up to 0.2 s later. The progress ladder
  runs every tick as before.
- **Counted** (`m0`): 405 asks in 9,177 locomotion ticks (was one a tick while walking). BotProf `loco.stuck`
  0.35-0.38 -> 0.07 ms a tick for the four brains.

### 3. The day cycle writes nothing in a frame that changes nothing (`31b7e98c`)

- **Was:** `DayCycle._process` (priority 1000) built the factors dictionary, parsed the file's colour strings,
  wrote five Environment values, two global shader values and the key light's colour, energy, fog energy and
  basis, and read each back, every frame of the match.
- **Now:** a frame is free when the hour, the camera's roof and depth shares are the ones last applied and every
  value it wrote is still there. Another script writing one of them (a zone's night value, a room switching the
  moon off, something turning the key light) makes the next frame a full one, which `test_day_cycle` already
  asked for. The roof / depth share lands on its target once within 0.001 of it. While the hour walks to the
  round's hour (80 s of every round) the light and air follow in steps of 0.002 h (0.03 degrees of sun; the sky's
  own step since T-259), and exactly once more on arrival.
- **Counted** (`m0`, the hour walking the whole window): full frames 2340 of 2340 -> 468; `day_cycle.gd`
  0.062 -> 0.026 ms a frame. With the hour arrived a frame costs the checks only (about 0.016 ms).
- **For the render side:** this also stops ten Environment / light writes a frame reaching the rendering server.

### 4. A hidden lamp does not flicker (`97d99d1c`)

`FlickerLight` computed three noise samples and wrote `light_energy` for lamps a room's cell has switched off.
Its clock still runs. `flicker_light.gd` on Totenhafen (79 lamps) 0.157 -> 0.102 ms a frame; on Map 0 (65, nearly
all lit at the spawn) no change.

### 5. `Game._process` asks for the tuning table only while a time effect runs (`8c02e2ac`)

The B&S autoload ran `Data.has_table("bs/tuning")` (`FileAccess.file_exists`: the file system) and a table
lookup every frame of every mode, for three scales it needs only during hitstop, slow motion or the weapon
wheel. `Engine.time_scale` is still written every frame. `game.gd` 0.10-0.11 ms a frame -> off the probe's list
(0.00 in all six runs of the final pairs).

### 6. The squad panel's rows are gathered 10 times a second (`1703ad2e`)

`CoopHud` built a dictionary per teammate and `SquadPanel` turned the list into text to see whether it changed,
every frame. Now every `ROWS_EVERY_S` (0.1 s); every frame while someone is down (the cross pulses).
`coop_hud.gd` 0.16-0.20 -> 0.05-0.06 ms a frame.

### 7. The inn's people and every face are posed every 4th frame out of view (`79663625`)

`PatronBody.lod_div` (the patrons, the survivors, the people, and `FaceDriver`'s blend shapes) takes
`Zombie.out_of_view` too. `patron_body.gd` (13 bodies, Map 0) 0.49-0.61 -> 0.41-0.46 ms a frame at the spawn,
0.53 -> 0.43 in a played round; the share depends on where the player looks.

### 8. No walk over the whole map for the errand planner's Interactables or a dropped power-up (`02967d1f`, fixed by `33521274`)

- **Was:** `Node.find_children` over Map 0's 12,700 nodes costs 3.7 ms on this desktop (`perf_marks --diag`).
  `BotEEHub.resolve` made one every 6 s for its pool of Interactables, inside whichever brain's think or hub
  frame asked first; `BotSquad` made one in the frame every power-up dropped.
- **Now:** Interactables join the group `interactables` in the base class's `_ready` and the pool is that
  group's members under the map, in the same tree order (unit-tested against `find_children`); the power-up is
  found in the pickups' own group. (The first version joined in `_init`. GDScript does not run a base `_init` for
  a subclass that has its own, so such a subclass would have been missed; a unit test written afterwards caught
  it and `33521274` moved it. Every Interactable subclass's `_ready` calls the base one: checked over all 75
  scripts and 7 inner classes.) The hub's knowledge poll and sight sweep run on two consecutive frames, not one
  (`bot_ee_hub.gd` p99 3.6 -> 2.7 ms on Totenhafen).
- **Not touched:** the hub's worst frame (13-22 ms: the Full Partner pick every second, stand points and path
  queries).

### 9. The sound bank is loaded in the background from the game's start (`f648ffa8`)

- **Was:** AudioDirector loads a sound on the main thread the first time it plays (2-30 ms each with a cold disk
  cache). T-237's warm-up loaded the SFX and Jingles one-shots on loader threads, but only from the first match's
  start, and left out the Ambience bus's one-shots (a gust, a stable creak, a drip: `map_ambience.gd`'s worst
  frames, 16-30 ms on four maps), the Music bus's one-shots (the round stingers) and the SFX bus's short loops
  (hums, engines, the crate).
- **Now:** everything but the looping Music / Ambience beds, queued when the game starts (+50 MB kept: 23 + 17
  + 10).
- **Counted** (`m0live`; perf_marks loads the map as the process starts, the worst case): sound files loaded in
  the frame that played them inside the sampled window: 0 (before: several a run, `stable_creak`,
  `amb_wind_gust`, `cellar_drip`, the round stinger). 17 remain, all in the process's first 150 physics frames
  (the map's beds, footsteps, two UI sounds), which a real session spends in the menu.

### 10. A voice line heard for the first time starts when a loader thread has brought its file (`62b5fdde`, `4c9f28e4`)

- **Was:** `GLVO.load_stream` loaded a line's `.ogg` on the main thread in the frame that started it: 4-12 ms on
  this desktop (10-50 ms with a busy disk) for each of the 2,681 lines the first time it is heard: the story
  voices, every speaker's first line, and the world's own speakers (the teller, the people, the inn's survivors,
  the pilot). It showed as 10-25 ms ticks under whatever script spoke (`zombie.gd` on a kill, `map_companion`,
  `bank_branch`, `inn_survivor`).
- **Now:** `GLVO.stream_ready(path)` asks a loader thread and the line waits for it: the part is handed back and
  asked for again next frame (the item stays the channel's current one; its subtitle, signal and queue place
  wait with it), `LOAD_WAIT_MAX_S` (0.5 s) at most, then the plain load as before. While it waits only the
  file is asked after (`4c9f28e4`: the first version worked the whole part out again every frame). The world
  speakers go through `GLVO.when_ready(path, callable)`.
- **Off** on the web (no loader threads) and, by default, in a headless run: the unit tests and smokes step the
  voice clock by hand and expect a line to start in the call that plays it. `perf_marks --vo-async` and the unit
  tests switch it on headless; **the windowed game has it on, and no smoke runs that path** (see "Checks run").
- **Counted** (`m0live` with `--vo-async`): 13-21 lines started late (a frame or a few), 1 voice file loaded in
  the frame that needed it (which one was not traced); before, 20-44 a run (10-18 of them over 2 ms).
- **Left:** the inn's opening script (it times itself by the length each line returns) and `play_external` (the
  Cousins' boss lines) still load in their frame.

### 11. The match's first kill no longer draws the blood decal's texture or compiles the dog tags' script (`287a96cc`)

Timed per listener of `GLBus.enemy_killed_at`: `HorrorLayer._on_killed_at` 25.8 ms on the first kill (the 128 x
128 blood decal texture drawn pixel by pixel in script the first time it is asked for; the mud one the same on
the first riser), `ScripBonuses._on_enemy_killed_at` 13.7 ms on the first kill that drops dog tags (`load()` of
`scrip_tag.gd`, a script nothing else names, compiled on the spot). The textures are now drawn when the map's
decal pool is built; `MapRoot.LATE_SCRIPTS` loads the dog tags' script and the local player's status overlay
before the match starts. After: neither listener over 1.5 ms on any kill of the same run, and `--diag` lists no
script, scene, shader, model or texture first loaded inside the window on any of the seven maps or in the live
round 30 (only voice files, on loader threads).

### 12. The column of the Returned sleeps while the camera does not look toward its lane (`d344f669`)

A dozen skinned bodies on Map 0's landscape were placed and animated every frame of the match. The lane is now
tested against the view cone as eight balls in a row; while all of it is out, nothing is placed and the
animation players rest, and the slept time is handed to the first frame it is looked at again. Counted: asleep
in 719 of 2340 frames at the spawn in one run (none in another: the lane lies partly ahead there), 844 of 4140
in a played round. Awake it was 0.16-0.20 ms of script a frame plus twelve animation players and skeletons.

### 13. A riderless horse at its rail is posed every 4th frame while unseen (`2a990756`)

The rig's procedural pose ran every frame (0.12-0.15 ms). Counted at Map 0's spawn (the rail is behind the
player): posed in 585 of 2340 frames. Ridden, mounting, walking home or in view: every frame.

### 14. A patron's pose weights are stepped only while one of them is on its way (`6eecfe0c`)

`PatronBody._process` built a list of ten pose names and stepped ten weights for every visible body every
frame. Now only until they have arrived. Counted: stepped in 2,388 of 19,920 body-frames (12 %).

## After

### What could be timed

By the time the last commit was in, the machine was three times slower than in the first hour (the same baseline
build's `m0` frame read 23-40 ms where it had read 12.3; the noise floor's worst tick 1-59 ms where it had been

## What a Steam Deck or an M1 would see (an estimate, not a measurement)

By the brief's rule of thumb a Steam Deck's CPU is 2.5 to 3 times slower per thread than this desktop and an
Apple M1 about 1.5 times. **Nothing below was run on either machine**: it is this desktop's quiet-machine numbers
multiplied.

Two things multiply on a slower machine:

1. Every millisecond of game logic here is 2.5-3 ms there.
2. The game's tick is 60 Hz whatever the frame rate. The per-tick part of the logic (the zombies' and the bots'
   `_physics_process`, the physics step) is paid 60 times a second however few frames are drawn; only the
   per-frame part (animation, HUD, ambience) is paid once a frame. So the question on a slow machine is first
   whether 60 ticks fit into a second at all, and then how many frames fit into what is left.

Per-tick part = the scripts' time per tick + the engine's step after it; per-frame part = the scripts' time per
process frame + the engine's work after it. Desktop numbers from the baseline (and after, by the measured
-4 % standing and -10 % played); Deck = x 2.5 to x 3.

| Scene | Desktop: per tick / per frame, ms | Deck: per tick, ms | Deck: 60 ticks take, of each second | Deck: frames a second the rest allows, before any drawing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Map 0 round 15, full squad, standing, before | 6.5 / 5.2 | 16-19.5 | 0.97-1.17 s | none: the tick alone does not fit; the game runs slow (4 ticks a frame at most) |
| the same, after | 6.3 / 5.1 | 15.8-18.9 | 0.95-1.13 s | none to 4 |
| Map 0 round 15, full squad, played, before | 8.8 / 6.4 | 22-26 | 1.3-1.6 s | none |
| the same, after | 8.1 / 6.0 | 20-24 | 1.2-1.46 s | none |
| Map 0 round 15 without the squad (the bots' scripts taken out of the baseline; not run) | about 3.5 / 5.0 | 9-10.5 | 0.53-0.63 s | 25-38 |
| A live round 30 (40 zombies), full squad, before | 9.0 / 8.2 | 22-27 | 1.35-1.6 s | none |

Read it as an order of magnitude, not a forecast: the rule of thumb is coarse and the headless frame has no
renderer in it. But it says plainly that **on a Deck-class CPU the game's own 60 Hz tick with a full squad and
24 zombies takes about a whole second per second**, and that this pass (4-10 % of the logic, depending on how
much of the horde is out of view) does not change that. What would, in order of size:

- **the squad**: each brain is 0.55-0.65 ms a tick here, 1.4-2 ms on a Deck; four of them are 40 % of the tick.
  Fewer bots, or cheaper ones, on a low preset is the largest single lever and is a setting, not a rewrite;
- **the physics engine**: Jolt measured about 5 % of the frame here (see "For the other workers"), less than the quarter of the tick that `move_and_slide` and the rays are;
- **a 30 Hz simulation on a low preset** halves the tick's share of the second outright. It is a gameplay
  change (the movement work of T-256 was tuned and tested on the 60 Hz tick) and needs the smokes run at 30 Hz;
- **cheaper zombie animation in view** on a low preset (`Zombie.anim_lod_scale`, as the web build does): the
  per-frame part.

On an M1 (x 1.5): Map 0 round 15 with the full squad is 9-10 ms a tick and 7-8 ms a frame, 17 ms together:
60 FPS is out of reach with the squad before any drawing, 40-45 is not. Without the squad it fits.

## What a player could notice

Nothing in gameplay changed: no timing, damage, hitbox, navmesh, prompt or AI decision rule. What a player
could in principle see or hear, most likely first:

1. **A voice line heard for the first time in a session starts a few frames later**, its subtitle with it (up to
   0.5 s if the disk is very slow, then it loads on the spot as before). Windowed game only.
2. **A zombie, patron or face coming into view from behind you** was posed up to 3 frames ago and is animated
   every frame from the frame it is seen. Its body was always in the right place. A zombie standing behind you
   that throws a shadow into your view animates that shadow at a quarter of the rate (15 a second at 60 FPS).
   A zombie's feet take a quarter of a second to settle onto a slope again after it comes into view.
3. **A zombie near the camera that is chasing someone far away** is now animated every frame (it was every 2nd or
   4th: the old rule went by the distance to its target); a horde round a bot far from the camera is animated at
   the far rate. This is a correction, visible in co-op and with bots.
4. **Footsteps of zombies behind you** come up to 3 frames (50 ms) late.
5. **The squad panel** (names, Scrip, tonics) shows a change up to 0.1 s later; at once while someone is down.
6. **The sun, the ambient light and the fog** follow the hour in steps of a 500th of an hour (six a second) for
   the 80 s it walks at the start of a round, not every frame.
7. **A bot stuck on a table or beside the navmesh** is set down up to 0.2 s later than before.
8. **The horse at the rail and the column of the Returned** hold their pose while nobody looks at them.
9. **Memory**: about 50 MB more sound kept loaded (the ambience one-shots, the stingers, the short loops).

## For the other workers

**T-264 (ports, presets, `project.godot`):**

- **The physics tick is 60 Hz whatever the frame rate, and `max_physics_steps_per_frame` is 4.** Below 60 FPS a
  frame runs more than one tick: at 30 FPS every frame pays the per-tick scripts twice (the zombies' and the
  bots' `_physics_process`, the physics step). The per-tick half of the script time is the larger half:
  5.4 of the 9.1 ms on Map 0 at round 15, and the physics step comes on top. The Deck estimate above counts this.
- **The physics engine.** `move_and_slide` and the ray queries are the largest single engine cost in the tick.
  Godot 4.7 ships Jolt (`physics/3d/physics_engine="Jolt Physics"`). Measured only, with the setting in a
  local `override.cfg` in a second worktree and nothing committed: two interleaved pairs of `m0`, each run's load
  taken out: `zombie.gd` -12 % (about -0.35 ms), the bots' own movement -12 to -17 % a call, the gap after the
  physics scripts 1.44 -> 1.09 ms; the scripts' total -8 % and -2 %. About 0.6-0.7 ms of a 12 ms frame: worth
  having on a Deck (1.5-2 ms there by the rule of thumb) but not the large lever it looked like. **How the game
  behaves under Jolt (character movement, ragdolls, the rays' hits, the smokes) was not looked at at all.**
- **Things a low preset could turn** (all exist as values; none is wired to a preset here, the presets are
  yours): `Zombie.anim_lod_scale` (WebProfile already halves the animation distances with it),
  `Zombie.VIEW_OUT_DIV` (a const now), the squad's size (each brain is 0.55-0.65 ms a tick on this desktop),
  `vfx_quality` (the horror layer's flies and decal pool already follow it).
- **`GLVO.async_start`** is off when `DisplayServer.get_name() == "headless"` and wherever `StreamWarm.enabled`
  is false (the web). A headless export would load voice files in their frame, as before.
- **The sound warm-up keeps about 150 MB of sound in memory** (100 MB since T-237, +50 MB now) and reads it in
  the first seconds after start: on a Deck's SD card that is a few seconds of background disk work in the menu.

**T-266 (render load):**

- Zombies, the inn's people and faces out of the camera's view are now animated at a quarter of the rate, so
  their skins and shadow casters change a quarter as often; a zombie behind the player still casts its shadow.
- `DayCycle` no longer writes five Environment values, two global shader values and the key light's four every
  frame: only when the hour, the roof share or another script's write changes them (every 5th frame while the

- `FlickerLight` still writes `light_energy` every frame for every lit lamp (65 on Map 0, 79 on Totenhafen), and
  `LampGlow` follows with material writes; `lamp_sway.gd` moves a shadowed lamp. Each write reaches the
  rendering server. Not touched (lights are yours).
- Running at round 15 on Map 0 (`perf_marks --diag`): 32 animation trees (the pool; inactive bodies do not
  advance), 12 animation players under MapAmbience (the column of the Returned: now asleep while its lane is out

- The time outside the scripts in a headless frame (no drawing at all) is 1.0-1.3 ms after the physics scripts
