# T-266: the render load of every map (2026-11-23)

The owner: "we need to optimize, we need to make this playable on macOS SteamOS. the frame rate still sucks at
times." This is the renderer's share: what each map draws per frame, counted, and what was taken off it. The ports
and the Handheld preset are T-264, the scripts' CPU cost is T-265. Branch `claude/perf-render`.

Counts are the evidence here (draw calls, triangles, shadow views, lamps), not milliseconds: the PC was shared with
five other workers, and the rendered runs are software rendering under WSL, where a frame takes ten seconds. What a
real GPU has to answer is listed at the end with the commands.

## How it was counted

- **The frustum census, headless** (`perf_marks --views=<capture sets>`, new): the player stands at every distinct
  camera of the map's capture sets (43 to 108 per map) at round 15 with 24 zombies in a block in front of the camera
  and the squad behind it, and the tool counts what stands in the camera's frustum from the scene tree: mesh
  instances, surfaces, skinned meshes, lamps, shadowed lamps and their shadow views under ShadowBudget, the sun's
  cascades, shadow casters, particle systems, decals, transparent surfaces, materials, shader variants. No occlusion:
  an upper bound, the same before and after. `--casters` lists the shadow casters by place and size and why a static
  batch still casts its own shadow; `--tree=<node>` shows what a prop is made of.
- **The heaviest views** of each map became the capture sets `t266_<key>` (and `t266_<key>_noon`, the day held at
  12:00, on the four maps with the day): `tools/perf/pick_views.py` takes the worst cameras by surfaces, lamps, lamp
  shadow draws, particles and transparent surfaces. The same cameras are counted rendered and captured.
- **The rendered census** (`tools/perf/render_census.sh`, Forward+ on lavapipe under WSL + Xvfb, invisible): the
  renderer's own numbers per view (`RenderingServer.viewport_get_render_info`): objects, draw calls and triangles in
  the colour pass and in the shadow passes apart. `--cheap` makes the pixels cheap without changing what is drawn
  (quarter-size picture, no MSAA / SDFGI / SSAO / SSR / fog, 256 px shadow atlases): draw calls, objects, lamps and
  shadow views are exact; triangles are for the quarter-size picture (mesh LODs pick for the smaller picture).
- **Before and after from one loaded map**: `RenderTune` (new) switches each pass of this task off and on in the
  running map, and `perf_marks --t266-ab` counts every view in every state before it moves to the next view, so a
  before / after pair is seconds apart (the maps live: weather, set pieces and patrols change the count by a hundred
  draw calls from one minute to the next, which made two separate runs useless for small differences).

## What was wrong

1. **ShadowProxies (T-070) had silently stopped working.** It merges the static batches' shadow geometry into a few
   depth-only meshes per 12 m cell, but only for "plain" materials, which meant StandardMaterial3D. Since the maps
   took the CC0 library's textured materials (ShaderMaterials on `surface_pbr.gdshader`), almost no batch counted:
   on Map 0 436 of 479 static batches drew their own shadow again, one draw call each in every shadow view in
   reach; Der Kranich's 183 batches were all left out because they have a draw distance; Thalbach's dressing
   batches, Groom Lake's baked dressing, Mondlicht's zone batches and the Inn's landscape batches were never
   covered. First rendered count on Map 0 at High, the view from the front road: **8,524 draw calls in the frame,
   5,806 of them in shadow passes** (4.3 M shadow triangles).
2. **Props draw into shadow maps surface by surface.** A tonic machine's body is one mesh of 22 materials, the C-47
   wreck's fuselage 10, a barricade board 3: after the batches were merged, Map 0's 910 loose casters still held
   1,650 surfaces, each a draw call in every shadow view the prop stands in.
3. **Nothing is culled behind walls.** Only the camera's frustum culls. In the Inn's vault, 3 m underground, 1,541
   mesh instances are in the frustum; at the foot of the mine's hall on Kaltenbrunn 1,689. ViewCells (T-145)
   answers this by hand, and only on Totenhafen and Mondlicht.
4. **The Shadows setting never touched the sun.** ShadowBudget (T-258) caps the lamps' shadow views (Low 4), but
   the sun or moon kept its four cascades over 70 m on every setting, so on Low the sun was most of the shadow work.
5. **No lamp has a draw distance.** 118 to 176 omni / spot lamps stand in the frustum in the heavy views of the Inn,
   Kaltenbrunn and Totenhafen; on Kaltenbrunn only 30 of 176 are within 50 m of the camera.

Looked at and found in order: the zombies (two surfaces and generated LODs each, animation LOD by distance), mesh
LODs (131 of 165 imported models generate LODs; the 34 without are the four wonder weapons' viewmodels and
animation-only files), particles (20 to 60 systems emitting per map, GoreFx's marks ring-buffered per kind and scaled
by `vfx_quality`), decals (under 20 a view).

## What changed

Every pass is attached by `MapRoot` at load (a runtime pass, like ShadowBudget): the generated `.tscn` files and the
generators are untouched, so regenerating a map loses nothing. `-- --t266-off[=proxies,props,occlusion,sun,lights]`
plays without them.

| Pass | Where | Acts on | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ShadowProxies, wider** | `gravelight/world/shadow_proxies.gd` | every setting | A ShaderMaterial counts as plain when its shader moves no vertex and writes no alpha, depth or discard (`code_cull` reads the source; `surface_pbr` passes). Batches with only a far cut-off (35 m and up) merge too and the proxy keeps the cut-off a cell further out. Batches tagged for ViewCells merge per group and the proxy carries the tag. `build_all` covers StaticBatches, DressBatches, ZoneBatches, Dressing/DressingBaked and Landscape/Batches. |
| **PropShadows** | `gravelight/world/prop_shadows.gd` | every setting | Every static prop mesh with two or more shadow-casting surfaces gets a shadow-only child with the same triangles as one surface per cull mode, and stops casting itself. The child sits under the mesh (moves, hides and frees with it) and takes its visibility range. Not touched: skinned meshes, blend shapes, anything on a character or rigid body, cut-out or vertex-moving materials. |
| **OcclusionWalls** | `gravelight/world/occlusion_walls.gd` | Level of detail Low, Medium | Occlusion culling. The occluders are the maps' own walls: the triangles of 1 m2 and more of the merged static batches (the proxies above), as children of their proxy. Only the player's own camera is culled this way; labels and sprites drawn through walls on purpose are exempt; doors, glass and everything that moves are no occluders. |
| **The sun and small casters** | `gravelight/world/shadow_budget.gd` (`SUN`) | Shadows Low, Medium | Low: the sun's cascades 4 -> 2 and its reach 70 -> 40 m, things under 50 cm cast no shadow. Medium: 2 cascades, 55 m, under 25 cm. |
| **LightRange** | `gravelight/world/light_range.gd` | Level of detail Low, Medium | A lamp fades out between 75 % and 100 % of its draw distance (Low 36 m, Medium 56 m, plus the lamp's own reach), its shadow with it. Lamps a character carries and lamps that already fade are left alone. |

Why High and Ultra look the same: the first two passes draw the same triangles with the same cull mode into the
same shadow maps (positions and normals are kept, so the lights' normal bias pushes them as before); the other
three do nothing on High and Ultra. Captures before / after: see "Proof".

## What remains heavy, and what it would take

- **The colour pass on High and Ultra is as it was.** Occlusion culling is only on for Low and Medium detail, so on
  High the Inn's front road still draws about 2,400 colour-pass draw calls and the underground views 1,700 to 2,100.
  It is the same pass that takes the cellar bay from 2,124 to 289 on Low. It was kept off High because a culling
  mistake hides things, and only captures of a few views in software rendering stand behind it so far. To take it:
  look at `-- --preset=high --occlusion` on the GPU on every map, then add "high" to `OcclusionWalls.LEVELS`.
- **Tonic machines and other many-material props in the colour pass.** One machine body is 22 materials, so 22
  draw calls, and a dozen machines stand in most frustums (about 400 surfaces a view on the Inn). PropShadows only
  fixes their shadows. The fix is in the art: fewer materials per GLB (an atlas per machine).
- **Lamps on High.** Every lamp of the map is still handed to the renderer on High and Ultra (176 on Kaltenbrunn).
  The CPU cost per lamp is small; what it costs a GPU per pixel is not known here. The next lever is per-room light
  culling (ViewCells cells for the Inn, Kaltenbrunn, Thalbach and Groom Lake; today only Totenhafen and Mondlicht
  have them), which is hand work per map.
- **The sun on High.** Four cascades, each drawing every caster in its slice. Left: sun-only proxies merged over
  larger cells (Light3D.shadow_caster_mask keeps them out of the lamps' maps), not done.
- **Shadow triangles went up on High** by 15 to 20 % (a merged proxy is culled per 12 m cell, not per batch, and a
  prop's shadow child has no mesh LODs). Fewer draw calls for more vertices is the right trade for a frame bound
  by the CPU; on a weak GPU at High it is the wrong one. On Low the shorter sun brings them below where they were.
- **Zombies.** 24 zombies are 140 to 250 surfaces in view and draw into every shadow view they stand in. Not
  touched: dropping far zombies' shadows on Low is a possible next step.
- **Transparent surfaces** (60 to 240 in the frustum: glass, fog cards, particles) and the volumetric fog are
  overdraw questions only a GPU answers.

## Shader and pipeline compile stalls

Not measured: a compile is counted per frame in play (`perf_marks --compiles`), and under software rendering a
frame takes ten seconds, so a round cannot be played there. What can be said from the code: `RenderWarm` (T-070)
draws the first-use materials (a severed piece, the stump cap, the wound decal, the glass effect) at load, and
systems add their own through `RenderWarm.add_provider`. This task adds no new first-use material: the shadow
proxies and prop shadow children all share one unshaded material per cull mode, drawn from the first frame.
The command for a GPU is in the list below.

## What only a real GPU can answer

All from the repo root after `. tools/env.sh`; on the owner's PC a rendered window needs the owner's OK
(off-screen `--position -4000,-4000`, silent). `<id>`: map00_crossroads, map01_kaltenbrunn, map02_totenhafen,
map04a_thalbach, map04b_kranich, map04c_groomlake, map04d_mondlicht.

1. **Frame rate before and after, per map and preset** (the owner's desktop, a Deck at 1280x800, a Mac):
   `"$GODOT" --audio-driver Dummy --position -4000,-4000 --resolution 1920x1080 --path . res://gravelight/tools_src/perf_marks.tscn -- --map=<id> --preset=<low|medium|high|ultra> --breakdown`
   and the same with `--t266-off` appended (the map as it was). `--round=30` for a late round, `--live` for a
   running round. The line to read: `[perf] in play: avg FPS ...` and `per frame (avg): ... render cpu ... gpu`.
2. **What each pass buys in milliseconds**: the same command with `--t266-off=proxies`, `--t266-off=props`,
   `--t266-off=occlusion`, `--t266-off=sun`, `--t266-off=lights` (one at a time).
3. **Whether occlusion culling pays on a slow CPU** (it rasterises the occluders on the CPU every frame): on a
   Deck, `--preset=low` against `--preset=low --t266-off=occlusion`.
4. **Occlusion on High, by eye**: play with `-- --occlusion` on High and walk every map looking for anything that
   pops in late at a doorway or a window; then decide whether High gets it.
5. **The shadow triangles on High**: `--preset=high` against `--preset=high --t266-off=proxies,props`, the `gpu`
   figure. If the GPU time rises on a Mac, the proxies' cell (ShadowProxies.CELL) should shrink.
6. **Compile stalls in a round**: `... perf_marks.tscn -- --map=<id> --preset=high --compiles --live --secs=120`
   lists every frame that compiled a pipeline and what entered the tree in it.
7. **The look on Low and Medium**: lamps fading out at 36 / 56 m, the sun's shadow ending at 40 / 55 m and small
   things without shadows: `Play.bat`, Settings > Video, on the Inn's yard and Kaltenbrunn's yard at noon (round 13).
8. **The rendered census on a GPU** (seconds instead of an hour, at full size, so the triangle counts are the real
   ones): `... perf_marks.tscn -- --map=<id> --preset=high --views=t266_<key> --t266-ab=proxies:props:occlusion+sun+lights`
   (`<key>`: m00, m01, m02, m04a, m04b, m04c, m04d).

triangles; the merge is now written by hand and a check counts both sides). The other maps' rendered numbers are
not tabled.

Also found, for T-264: on the Compatibility renderer the WebProfile sets keep omni lamps on dual-paraboloid shadows,
which Godot 4.7 does not support there (2,534 "Dual paraboloid shadow mode not supported in the Compatibility
renderer" errors in one capture run); those lamps probably cast no shadow in a Compatibility build. Not fixed.
