# TERMS & CONDITIONS: Canon Bible

This is the single source of story truth for all three stories. When another document disagrees with this one, this one wins. If this one is wrong, fix it here first, then everywhere else. Quick-reference facts (IDs, dates, numbers) live in `M0_SHARED_FACTS.md`. The dated chronology lives in `TIMELINE.md`.

**The theme:** nobody reads the fine print. In Bolt & Sprocket, a megacorp conquers a galaxy one free trial at a time. In Gravelight, the dead of 1944 come from a sickness a thousand years sealed that men broke open in 1930, and the perk machines, the points and the cheerful voice over them turn out to be an alien company that came to sell to it. The Fine Print shows the player both sides.

**The working title** is TERMS & CONDITIONS, stored in `Game.GAME_TITLE` (`core/game.gd`).

## 0. The Lazarus origin (UPDATE 11, DECISIONS T-190; the full bible is `docs/LAZARUS.md`)

**Where the dead come from.** Not from an alien company. From **a sickness kept for a thousand years in a sealed vessel**
that travelled with the relics of Lazarus of Bethany, the man four days dead who was called out of the tomb (John 11).
Orthodox tradition calls him Lazarus the Four-Days-Dead; his relics went from Kition on Cyprus to Constantinople under
Leo VI (898, some say 890). **Our invention, never presented as history or Church teaching:** a small lead-and-glass
vessel went with the relics and was kept apart from them. The monks who guarded it wrote that it held *"what death left
behind when it gave him back"*: the debt death was owed and never collected. When Constantinople fell in 1453 three of
them carried it north to Rus. For almost five hundred years it sat sealed in the crypt of the **Church of St Lazarus the
Four-Days-Dead** in the village of **Chetverodnevo** (fictional), under a lamp a brotherhood of watchers never let go
out, behind one line cut in Church Slavonic over the crypt door: **"What was closed on the fourth day, let no one open."**

**The miracle is the gift. The vessel is the debt. They are never the same thing.** Faith is never the villain; the
miracle is never mocked, faked or called a disease; the monks and Father Serafim are the most decent people in the saga.
The horror is entirely what men did when they broke the seal.

**Lazarus Saturday, 12 April 1930 (30 March Old Style).** In the anti-religious campaign a district brigade comes to close
the church: the bell roped down and cracked, the tower dynamited, the crypt broken open for silver. The priest, **Father
Serafim Lebedev**, refuses the keys and is set aside. A young OGPU officer, **Arkady Volkov**, opens the vessel on the
altar step to prove there is nothing in it. **The blood inside is still wet.** The sickness keeps its own calendar: **the
dead rise on the fourth day.** Three of the brigade fall ill and die in the district fever ward; on the fourth day they
get up, and so does everyone they bit, and so does the village, because Father Serafim buried the brigade's first
victims himself. The OGPU seals the district as a typhus quarantine. Chetverodnevo is erased from the map. Volkov, never
bitten, carries the vessel to Moscow in a lead case.

**Laboratory No. 4, "FOURTH DAY" (ЧЕТВЁРТЫЙ ДЕНЬ).** From July 1934 (the NKVD exists from then) the vessel is a secret
NKVD programme under Volkov, who rises through the purges by being useful. Its files call the sickness **"Lazar"**. What
they learn: it lives in blood; the dead rise on the fourth day; the risen spread it by bite; **it cannot be made to
obey.** Its test subjects were camp prisoners, handled in the game only as documents, empty bunks and a list of names,
never as zombies and never as spectacle. By 1939 it is a weapon of last resort in a vault.

**Darkmatter's smaller job.** The 1930 awakening was visible across the galaxy as a *"natural retention phenomenon"*:
for a company that sells retention, the most valuable thing a market can have. **That is why Darkmatter comes to Earth.**
Its 1938 probe lands in the Kaltenbrunn salt as before, but its crystal, **Nachtglas**, does not make the dead rise:
it *answers the sickness*, so that near it the sickness wakes faster and runs further. The probe carried a **culture of
the sickness** with the crystal, "returned inventory" taken from Laboratory No. 4 through a fold and backdated (§2,
Next-Era Delivery™). That is why Jan Pilch rose in the mine in November 1938, and why Vendelin saw it (The Fine Print
Ch. 2, kept). **Darkmatter found the dead, packaged the sickness with its crystal, and sells to it.** It explains only
the technology: the perk machines (§4), the Crucible (§5), the Ledger and Scrip, the Operator, the crate and Nachtglas.
Everything in §2–§5 stands with that one change of verb.

**Kaltenbrunn.** Projekt Wiederkehr (1939 →) studies the crystal and the miners it raised; Reinholt wants Liesl back;
**Lazarus** is the name her grief hangs on, and her programme's name for the sickness is **Lazarusblut**. The perks, the
Ledger and the Crucible come in through Helvetia Automaten AG as built.

**October 1944: the Soviets unleash it.** The Red Army's first attack into East Prussia (the Gumbinnen–Goldap operation,
16–30 October, real) stalls; in our history Moscow sends Volkov and the last-resort order. FOURTH DAY is released by shell
and by infected volunteers' blood. The German line breaks. Four days later **the Red Army's own dead get up**, thousands,
from field graves, aid stations and the snow; they do not know whose side they were on and **they walk west**. The
soldiers call them **the Returned** (вернувшиеся). Moscow calls it *"an epidemic among the enemy"*. The line broke and
kept breaking: **the distant war seen from the Crossroads Inn is the front where the Soviet dead are walking**, and the
Red Army is fighting both.

**The Germans make it airborne.** Infected Soviet dead go by train to Kaltenbrunn (Fasbender saw them come in). Reinholt's
laboratory finds that near Nachtglas the sickness takes **minutes**, not days, and that ground fine with the crystal into
a violet dust **it carries on the air**: **STAUB** ("dust"). From November 1944 Abteilung III sells the troops the Marke
(§2.2, unchanged) as *protection against the Lazarus sickness*; it is not protection, it is a Nachtglas tag that makes the
marked rise on command and walk where the relay points them. **20 December 1944, the Erwachen, was the first STAUB
release**, aimed east; the wind turned and blew the dust back over Kaltenbrunn, the villages and the road garrison, the
same night Reinholt drove the Heart to full resonance to reach Liesl. Both are true at once: every body for thirty
kilometres rose, Soviet and German alike. Three nights later the same Heart brought down GRAVELIGHT ONE (unchanged).

**Through the maps.** Map 0's dead are villagers, the marked road garrison and Soviet risen in greatcoats out of the
east; Kaltenbrunn holds the STAUB plant and Reinholt's records; Totenhafen's lead coffins hold Lazarusblut and STAUB
consigned to "Hale, L."; Helvetia Works is unchanged (the technology crossover); Morgenrot's dawn salvo carries STAUB to
London, Antwerp and Paris (cities and egg unchanged); the Kranich's bombs carry STAUB across the Atlantic, Groom Lake's
programme is built on samples Peg's orders bought, and the Moon relay is what keeps the sickness awake worldwide and the
marked obedient. **CANCEL makes the dead lie down, as built.** After the canon ending, one more card: **Moscow, 1953**, a
lead case in a vault under Laboratory No. 4, an old man's hand on it, the vessel still wet.

**The people.** Father Serafim's diary is the thread: one Archive page per map, in order; he is never a zombie, his fate
is one line. Volkov is in the documents on every map and in the October 1944 cutscene, never a cartoon. The squad keep
their secrets and arcs (§6.2): Deacon is the saga's quiet theological voice, a line at a time, no sermons; Wren knows the
NKVD; Dutch's grandmother's letter is about the glass in the salt, as now; **Peg's orders now say retrieve samples of
the sickness and the crystal**; Jakub is unchanged; Fasbender is more haunted.

**Names:** Lazarusblut (German, the sickness) · STAUB (the airborne programme) · ЧЕТВЁРТЫЙ ДЕНЬ / FOURTH DAY (the Soviet
programme) · "Lazar" (the Soviet files' word) · Gänger, from Wiedergänger (stays the German word for the risen) · the
Returned, вернувшиеся (the Red Army's own risen). The full table, the real/invented table and the sources are in
`docs/LAZARUS.md` §7, §11, §12. No real person is a villain or a victim by name.

## 1. The two worlds

### 1.1 The Bolt & Sprocket galaxy (the Rustbelt galaxy, far future)
A cartoon-bright, comedic, far-future galaxy of junk planets, orbital malls and resort worlds.

**OmniScrap™**, run by **Chairman Grindstone**, has turned everything into a subscription: air (AIR PREMIUM™), lava (LAVA: NOW LEGALLY WATER), sunsets ("almost monetized"), freezing people for later (ForeverYou™), and a whole smart city fined until it emptied (Nova Plaza). Its motto, in Grindstone's mouth, is "Everything is a subscription, boy."

**Its people:**
- **Bolt** is a junk-planet mechanic whose garage is repossessed under Section 12-B ("Two mallets and a sandwich!").
- **Sprocket** is his floating, one-eyed, four-mood robot. He was rebuilt from a refund bin, and he carries OmniScrap's **Clause 44** (remote decommission) in his firmware.

Together they take OmniScrap down, planet by planet. The whole story is told through dialogue, holo-calls, memos and ad vignettes that are already canon (`data/bs/story/`).

**OmniScrap's empire "ran on meeting minutes."** Sprocket's last act of the campaign is forwarding all 35 internal memos (40 in the remake) to forty billion inboxes. OmniScrap stock is "reclassified as confetti."

### 1.2 Earth, 1938–1945
Grim pulp horror with gallows humor: a lost war film that slowly becomes a ghost story.

**Nachtglas** is a black, glassy mineral with violet-amber veins, recovered in 1938 from a "meteorite" strike in the Kaltenbrunn salt mine in occupied Silesia. It does not raise the dead by itself: it *answers the sickness* (§0). A body that carries the sickness and dies near it keeps running its last impulse, in minutes instead of days, and in the end that impulse is hunger. Living tissue dosed with diluted Nachtglas gains temporary traits.

**Projekt Wiederkehr** ("Project Return") is the Reich program built on the mineral. Its lead scientist, **Dr. Augusta Reinholt**, privately wants her daughter **Liesl** back; Liesl died of diphtheria in 1939. Her colleague **Dr. Emil Fasbender** understood what the material really does and offered to defect.

**Operation Gravelight** is the joint SOE/OSS extraction team sent for Fasbender, made up of Dutch, Peg, Deacon and Wren. Their plane is brought down near Kaltenbrunn on 23 December 1944 by "something that isn't flak."

The full Gravelight canon lives in `docs/gravelight/` (LORE, CHARACTERS, ENEMIES, WEAPONS, MAPS, EASTER_EGGS, VO_SCRIPT), amended by brief §11.

## 2. What connects them: Darkmatter Subscriptions LLC

The original game ends with Darkmatter as sequel bait (`cs.postCredits.01–03`):
- Darkmatter reviews OmniScrap's leaked memos and sees "a market gap of approximately forty billion souls."
- It orders an acquisition, and wants whoever has been reading the internal memos "on OUR payroll."
- Sprocket then gets a calendar invite for a "synergy screening" in another galaxy, sent *yesterday*.

**Darkmatter is the hidden source of every machine in Gravelight, and of none of its dead** (§0). It found the dead,
packaged the sickness with its crystal, and sells to it.

- **Who they are:** a megacorp from the neighboring **Umbral Galaxy**. Their motto is **"NOBODY CANCELS."** OmniScrap sold subscriptions; Darkmatter sells **retention**. Grindstone was "just the invoice." Darkmatter is the collections department.
- **Brand:** deep violet `#c07dff` with amber cracks. The original game already gave the Darkmatter speaker this violet.
- **Next-Era Delivery™:** Darkmatter ships product through folds in space *and time*, using a resonant crystal it sells as **DARK-GLASS™**. Delivery ignores chronology, so Darkmatter can sell things to 1944 that it acquired "later" in the B&S timeline. Sprocket: *"They shipped it to the PAST. They BACKDATED THE INVOICE. That is the most Darkmatter sentence I have ever said."*
- **DARK-GLASS™ = Nachtglas.** On Earth, the Germans named the crystal Nachtglas.
- **The Sol-3 Test Market:** Darkmatter's Emerging Markets Division saw the awakening of April 1930 from across the galaxy as a *"natural retention phenomenon"* on an unlisted world, and wanted to know whether a "pre-subscription species" on which death was already not a cancellation event could be sold to.
  - On **9 November 1938** it fired test-market probe **TM-SOL3-001** through a fold. The probe landed in the Kaltenbrunn salt mine, and everyone took it for a meteorite.
  - The payload was DARK-GLASS™ **retention substrate**, and with it a **culture of the sickness** as "returned inventory" (lot LZR-04, origin Laboratory No. 4, Moscow, acquired by fold and backdated). The substrate's product function is *churn prevention*: it answers the sickness, so a body carrying both can be reached, counted and kept. A retained user cannot cancel.
  - A body with the sickness in it that dies near the substrate keeps running its last impulse. **The zombies are, to Darkmatter, the ultimate retained customers.** Darkmatter did not make them. It came for them. That is the horror of The Fine Print; the horror's *origin* is §0.
  - The probe's internal results line reads: **"EXCEPTIONAL RETENTION."**

### 2.1 Darkmatter's people
- **Sable Vantage** is Darkmatter's VP of Emerging Markets and launched the Sol-3 test market. She has a velvet voice and perfect posture, and says "we" when she means "you."
  - She is the Darkmatter representative who offered Reinholt her dead daughter back, *as a service*.
  - In Gravelight she is never named; Reinholt's recordings call her only **"the Woman in Violet."**
  - The player first *sees* her in The Fine Print Ch. 1's last scene, and fights her mech, THE QUARTERLY, in Ch. 4.
- **The Underwriter** is Darkmatter's risk-assessment enforcer: a towering, ledger-faced machine that "prices" everything it looks at. It enforces **Clause 0** and is The Fine Print's main boss.
- **The Board** is a conference call of distorted voices that is never shown. It is the voice of the B&S post-credits ("Initiate acquisition").
- **Vendelin** is Field Merchandiser Unit **V-1** (serial DMS-FMU-V1-0001). His directive is **"DISTRIBUTE. RETAIN. REPORT."**
  - He crash-landed with the probe in 1938, damaged and out of contact.
  - He passed as human: **Herr Viktor Vendelin, Ingenieur, Basel.**
  - He founded **Helvetia Automaten AG** in 1940, built every perk cabinet, and wrote every jingle. In his words, "a product must be *loved* to be retained." He grew to hate how catchy they are.
  - He learned from humans, understood what his product does to them, and sabotaged it from inside.
  - His recall clause is **Clause 0: "Field units are inventory."**

### 2.2 The Marke (UPDATE 09 §1, DECISIONS T-137; binding text in `docs/U09_CONTRACTS.md` C1)
**What it is.** Raw Nachtglas retains whatever dies near it with the sickness in it (Pilch in 1938, the Erwachen in the mine; §0). From 1942
**Projekt Wiederkehr's Abteilung III** (Venner's formulation department, the same people as the tonics) cut the substrate
into chips the size of a grain of rice, the **Marken** (singular *Marke*; player-facing "the mark"), and implanted them
under the skin of the **right hand**, between thumb and forefinger, or the **forehead**, above the right brow. It was sold
to soldiers, guards, staff and volunteers as a loyalty and identity tag: **"die Marke, die dich hält"** (the mark that
keeps you). Pay, rations and facility Scrip were tallied against it; the Ledger counts the marked; Vendelin's cabinets
read it; the Operator calls a marked person "a valued customer". It is Darkmatter's subscription token in the flesh.
**(UPDATE 11, T-190) From November 1944 it was sold with a second reason:** the poster gained the line *Schutz gegen die
Lazaruskrankheit*, protection against the Lazarus sickness the Soviet dead had brought west (§0). It is not protection.
A marked body carries a grain of the crystal that answers the sickness, so when it dies it rises in minutes, **on
command**, and walks where the relay points it: the one thing Laboratory No. 4 said could not be done. Everything else
in this section stands.

**Who got it.** Major Anselm Brück took the first one in front of the garrison in August 1942 ("for morale"); the guard
roster was a list of the marked by that Christmas (Lenz's duty roster, `INT_M1_19`, is that list). Abteilung staff,
the 1943–44 field-trial soldiers (which is why eleven of them rose on the Eastern Front, far from any ore), the
Totenhafen detachment and U-1047's ratings, Werk Thalbach's works guard. Kessler took it. Ammer took it and regrets it.
**Who refused it.** The forced labourers were never offered it. In May 1943 the overseers of the labour gangs were
offered the tag and the few who were asked refused; **Jakub Ostrowski** refused it in Gallery Nine ("I have a name. It
is enough to carry."), which is why he was never one of them and is alive. Sister Agnieszka was never marked.
Fasbender refused it and was marked down for it.

**What it does.** A marked person who dies does not stay dead: the Marke keeps the body running on its last impulse,
which is hunger: "NOBODY CANCELS". The marks stay active only while the relay signal reaches them: the **Ledger's
carrier** on Earth, Darkmatter's **Regional Retention Relay RR-7** on the Moon above it (`MAP4_FERNFLUG.md` §6). That is
why cutting the relay in the Fernflug finale stops the dead: the hum stops, the first retained miner's hand stops, and
every Marke goes dark the same second. Unmarked people who die near raw substrate can still rise (the mine's Erwachen,
the sealed galleries, the dosed subjects of the Laboratory Wing); far from the mine, everything that rises was marked, with one exception: every place the squad fights is a Darkmatter site (the cabinets, the Crucible, the coffins in the pens, the impellers, the Nevada stone, the relay), and the substrate in it is enough that whoever dies inside the site's reach rises too, Marke or not (that is how the relief squads' Allied dead rise, T-142); the Marke is what makes a body rise *anywhere*.
**The Erwachen of 20 December 1944** was both at once: the mine's raw substrate driven to full resonance, and every
marked guard who died that night getting up.

**Who knows what.** The squad never learns the corporate truth (§8): to them the mark is a thing the Germans put under
their skin that will not let them die. **Deacon**, a preacher's son, names it from memory ("the mark in the right hand,
or in the forehead"), quietly, not as a sermon. **Peg's** sealed orders say "retrieve the tokens" (Directive LANTERN §1
as amended; she does not know what they are for). **Wren** keeps finding them on the dead ("same hand, every time").
**Dutch** is blunt. **Fasbender** explains the carrier: the mark listens for a signal; silence it and they lie down.
**The Operator** calls a marked kill "a valued customer, retained."

**In play** (contract C1): every Gänger shows a faint violet glint under the skin of the right hand (60%) or the forehead
(40%), pulsing with the infection shader; it is a weak point (×1.5, a small Scrip bonus for a kill from it, none in
Classic Rules). Bosses and Darkmatter's constructs (the Backorder) have none. **Respect rule:** the Marke is never a
camp tattoo or a number; it is a violet glint under the skin, offered, never forced on the labourers; forced
labourers were not marked.

## 5. The Crucible's true origin (a chain the player can trace)

1. **B&S campaign:** Blartz invents the **Omega Forge**, which is why B&S weapons can evolve. The patent is BLZ-40417, the same serial as his PATENT PENDING gun.
2. **B&S campaign:** OmniScrap arrests Blartz for "unlicensed innovation" and **confiscates the prototype schematics**. In the remake this is shown in a new memo (#37) and a short Holding Block scene. When he's rescued, Blartz rebuilds the Forge "from memory, which is better than the original, which was also perfect."
3. **After OmniScrap collapses:** Darkmatter buys the confiscated IP in the fire sale and builds **Crucible Mod. R-E**.
4. **1944:** Via Next-Era Delivery, a Crucible is shipped to the Sol-3 test market. That is why Kaltenbrunn's Core Vault has one, plaque and all. Players can read the plaque in Gravelight long before they understand it.
5. **Fine Print Ch. 4:** Blartz learns the truth and is outraged, mostly about the color: *"They put MY forge in a WAR? And they made it VIOLET? Tacky."*
6. **Helvetia Works:** Blartz's original Omega Forge prototype, shipped to Basel as a reference unit, allows a **second upgrade tier** (Crucible variant → **Omega** variant, one weapon per match). Blartz's voice line comes through the Operator's speakers, and the Operator does not approve.

### 6.4 Jakub Ostrowski, the prisoner companion (Update 02 §6.2, DECISIONS T-051)
**Respect rule (v2 §0.1):** Jakub is a person and a hero. He is never a joke, never a zombie, never spectacle. In
play, a bled-out Jakub is out cold, never dead; he comes back on the next map ("Not yet. Not tonight.").

**Who he is.** **Jakub Ostrowski**, born 1901 in **Wilcza Góra**, the valley village below Kaltenbrunn (the Nowaks' village,
and Dutch's Babcia's). A hewer at the Kaltenbrunn salt works from 1919, on the civilian upper levels. When the Amt für
Sonderforschung took the mine (1939) the Polish miners were kept on; in **March 1941** their pay stopped and their families'
ration cards were held against them, and they became forced labourers in all but the word (tag **KB-2210**). In **October
1942**, when the forced labour moved into the deep galleries, he was sent down to **Gallery Nine** the same month the
occupation took **Tomasz Nowak** (KB-4471), Wren's brother, and he was Tomasz's gang leader for five months. In the spring of
1943 the doctors came for "young men with good lungs" and took Tomasz to the Laboratory Wing; Jakub never saw him again
(consistent with Agnieszka's note, CHARACTERS.md Wren). In June 1943 he was on the night shift when Mahlknecht locked the
gates on Gallery Seven: his shift lived. Wife **Marta**, daughter **Hania** (b. 1928), both in Wilcza Góra; a one-eared dog,
Burek. He knew the Nowak children as the timberman's two down the lane (Zosia shot the crows off his roof at nine). He
sold the Kreuzweg its winter coal before the war. No relation to Dr. Hanne Ostrowski of the Marathon Malt trials
(Ostrowski is a common name).

**The chalk.** Wilcza Góra's timbermen marked props with chalk; at Kaltenbrunn Jakub's gangs turned the marks into a quiet
alphabet for sabotage. **Three strokes and a hook** means "weak, ours, don't trust it": a prop left loose, a load stacked to
slip, a fuse that won't. The guards took it for a shift tally. He taught it to the young ones, Tomasz among them, who made
his hook **backwards**, always. His own marks are on the props in Gallery Nine and on the rail of the Maintenance Stairs.

**The escape (20–22 December 1944).** On the night of the Erwachen (20 Dec) the bell rang and did not stop and the guards ran.
Eleven men went up the old Maintenance Stairs, as planned since the spring; the guards on the third landing were still
shooting; slow (a bad knee) was careful, and careful was alive: he alone reached the snow (21 Dec) and said their names at
the top. He took a greatcoat off the guardroom hook (two sizes too big; he cut the badges off with a shovel blade, so it
carries no insignia) and kept the hook of the miner's lamp they had taken from him. On the 22nd a retreating road patrol
caught him on the Kaltenbrunn road, tagged the padlock "Häftling (Pole) · Flucht aus K. · 22.XII.", locked him in the
Crossroads Inn's coal cage, heard something on the road behind them and fled with the key. In the cage he wrote to Marta on
the back of a coal bill (the Archive's `INT_JAK_01`).

**Map 0 (23–24 Dec).** Round 5: a Gänger tears in through the coal chute and bursts the cage hinge trying to reach him; he
kills it with the coal shovel. The survivor who comes down chooses: give him a gun, or not yet. He follows that survivor.
He refuses perks ("I've seen what they put in those": he carried the cabinets' crates at Kaltenbrunn). He knows the
Cousins' rhyme: every schoolyard in the valley claps it; he sold Lene Vogt coal, and remembers her nieces small.
**Wren:** the first time she talks to him, the quiet scene (VO_SCRIPT §16.5): Tomek, the rats, the Laboratory Wing, the
backwards hook.

**Map 1, Kaltenbrunn (25 Dec).** He walks back into the place he walked out of four days before, as the squad's guide: his
mine lines per zone, the Maintenance Stairs' rusted lock (500 Scrip less while he is with you), his own chalk mark on the
stair rail. **The Return Trip (CANON §9):** he does not step into the fold. He stays at the rail siding at dawn to keep
Fasbender from running ("somebody has to"), so the squad finds both of them there when Basel returns them; he is not in
Helvetia Works.

**Map 2, Totenhafen (Jan 1945).** He comes to the coast because Wren goes, and because the Kaltenbrunn transfer lists name the
Baltic pens. His first sea. The pens were poured by hand by men like him; he sees it in the walls.

**[PLAN] Map 3, Morgenrot (April 1945): his arc's end.** The men transferred from Kaltenbrunn to the Hohe Wacht carried his
chalk alphabet with them: the **Witnesses' Marks** (v2 §4.2 step 3) are written in it. With Jakub in the match:
- each mark he stands by gets his reading in a second line, low and plain ("Three strokes and a hook. This weld is ours.
  Don't trust it."), so the marks' meaning (the weld, the gyro, the valve) is spoken by someone who made them;
- **Tomasz's mark** (always one of the six) is a **backwards hook**. Jakub sees it first and says nothing; he goes and stands
  by Wren while she finds it (her scene, `WRN_M3_TOMASZ_*`, with his one line at the end). The mark does not answer what
  happened to Tomasz between the Laboratory Wing and the transfer list; it only proves he held chalk at the Hohe Wacht.
  (The specimen log, Agnieszka's release and the transfer list stay the open question CANON already leaves open.)
- The forced labourers are never zombies here either: Jakub's lines are about the living men who left the marks;
- **ABORT ending (canon):** he does not take the last cable car. At dawn he walks down the mountain road toward Sankt
  Aurel with the handful of labourers the squad found alive in the Depot, leading them out the way he led eleven men up
  the stairs, and this time all of them reach the snow. His last line, to Wren: "It is a long way to Wilcza Góra. I have
  walked worse." The epilogue card: Marta's reply, one line, dated May 1945.
- He never dies in canon. In LAUNCH (non-canon) the card says only that he was seen walking north.

## 7. Mirror themes (use them deliberately)
- **Sprocket and Vendelin are mirrors.** Sprocket is an OmniScrap unit with Clause 44 who chose friendship over his firmware. Vendelin is a Darkmatter unit whose Clause 0 is enforced in The Fine Print. Bolt saved Sprocket; Bolt and Sprocket try to save Vendelin. The Ch. 5 finale mirrors `cs.l3_brick` beat for beat, with Sprocket now in Bolt's role: *"Friendship isn't a feature. It's a bug. And nobody gets to patch yours."*
- **Grindstone and Darkmatter:** Grindstone was "just the invoice." Darkmatter is the collections department.
- **Reinholt** is a Darkmatter customer: the saddest possible user of a retention product.
- **Memos:** OmniScrap fell because its memos got read. Darkmatter's secret is that it *writes* the fine print and counts on nobody reading it. The Fine Print Fernet perk and the back-of-machine panels are Vendelin's attempt to make people read.

## 8. Tone rules
- **Bolt & Sprocket** stays comedic satire. Darkmatter is seeded only lightly: 5 hidden memos (36–40), the derelict TONIQ™ dispenser in the Galleria, and one Retention Specialist line.
- **Gravelight** stays grim. **Gravelight characters never learn the word "subscription."** To them this is cosmic horror. The corporate truth surfaces only as the Operator's eerie slips. Dramatic irony is the point: the player knows and the soldiers don't.
- **The Fine Print** is where the tones meet. Corporate horror is played straight, with Bolt & Sprocket comedy on one side and a quiet, sad robot in 1940s Basel on the other.
- **Real history** (occupation, forced labor) is handled with respect: no jokes at victims' expense, and fictional places and companies wherever a real one could be implicated.
- **The Marke** (§2.2) is handled with the same respect: never a camp tattoo, never a number, never on a forced labourer. The labourers were never marked and the few who were offered it refused. It was *sold*, to people who had a choice, and the horror is that they took it. In text it is "the mark" or "die Marke", a violet glint under the skin, and nothing else.
- **The Lazarus layer** (§0, `docs/LAZARUS.md` §10): faith is never the villain; the miracle is never mocked, faked or called a disease (the sickness is the debt, not the gift); the clergy and the monks are the most decent people in the saga; real history is accurate where used and labelled fiction where not; no real person is a villain or a victim by name; Soviet and German soldiers are people; camp prisoners and forced labourers are never zombies and never spectacle; no real Nazi symbols, and no political imagery for shock. The horror comes from what people did.

## 9. The Return Trip (how the crossover fits the timeline)
At the end of Gravelight Map 1, the collapsing Nachtglas heart opens a fold. The squad steps through it into **Basel on the night of 17–18 March 1945**, which is The Fine Print Ch. 5, HELVETIA WORKS.

Meanwhile, Blartz's misfired delivery pad (end of Ch. 4) drops Bolt & Sprocket onto Vendelin's last known coordinates, the same night.

When Vendelin overrides Clause 0 and **cuts Darkmatter's fold**, the fold **returns its contents to sender**:
- Bolt & Sprocket are pulled home to the far future.
- The squad is put back in **late December 1944**, still within their one impossible night.

The squad later remembers Basel as a fever dream, and the dates in Peg's notebook don't add up. Map 2 Totenhafen (January 1945, built) follows as written; Map 3 is now **Morgenrot, April 1945** (`docs/MAP3_MORGENROT.md`, v2): Projekt Wiederkehr's dawn salvo from the Hohe Wacht, the Sonnenspeer carrying Reinholt's fragment as her payment for Liesl, the Operator calling it "the regional rollout". Its canon ending is ABORT; the LAUNCH ending is non-canon; the MOON ending leaves a violet smile on the Moon and a light that blinks back ("MONDLICHT", the Season Two hook). Vendelin stays in 1945 to help shut the substrate down, which leaves him alive in the world of Map 3. Canon rules for the map (v2 §0): the forced laborers who built the rockets are never zombies and are the reason the player wins; no real Nazi symbols anywhere (the fictional Sunburst-and-Split-Bell, `docs/ART_FACTIONS.md`); civilians only as skylines and sirens; rocket detail at placard level.
